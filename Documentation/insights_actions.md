# Hotel Insights & Recommended Actions
**Analysis period:** July 2015 – August 2017

---

## Resort Hotel

**Total Revenue:** €11.45M  
**Cancellation Rate:** 28%  
**Average Daily Rate:** €96.16  
**Average Stay:** 4.23 days  

---

### 1. Corporate Bookings — Focus on Autumn

Corporate bookings contribute just **2.92% of total revenue (€335k)**, but they are heavily concentrated in **Autumn**, which accounts for **56% of all corporate revenue**. This aligns with a quieter trading period for the resort, when leisure demand softens and capacity is available.

Corporate bookings are also **more reliable**, with a **22% cancellation rate** compared to **28% overall**, and typically bypass OTA commissions. This makes them lower-risk, higher-quality revenue arriving at the optimal time of year.

**Recommended actions**
- Develop meetings and retreat packages for **September–November**
- Begin proactive corporate sales outreach in **Q2–Q3**
- Introduce midweek corporate pricing tiers

**Expected outcome:** Improved Autumn occupancy & reduced revenue volatility

---

### 2. Ireland & Great Britain — Shoulder Season Strength

Ireland is a clear outlier, generating **€931k from 3,333 guests**, around **five times more** than any other high-spend country. Great Britain shows a similar pattern, combining strong spend per guest with high guest volumes.

Both markets perform well **outside peak summer**. Spring and Autumn bookings from Ireland and Great Britain together account for **nearly 10% of total resort revenue**, demonstrating demand that is not strongly seasonal. Ireland also has relatively high direct booking rates (almost 20% or 633 bookings) with direct bookings having a  much lower cancellation rate of 17% and avoid paying commission to online and offline travel agents.

**Recommended actions**
- Run targeted Facebook and Instagram campaigns for Irish and UK audiences:
  - **Jan–Mar** for Spring travel
  - **Jun–Aug** for Autumn travel
- Promote “shoulder season escapes” via email
- Partner with Irish and UK travel bloggers or influencers

**Expected outcome:** Stronger Spring and Autumn performance without discounting, and reduced reliance on OTAs.

---

### 3. Agent & Channel Concentration Risk

Revenue is highly concentrated. One agent alone contributes **€3.58M** or 31% of total revenue, while a single company accounts for **€560k**. This creates meaningful commercial risk.

If one of these partners reduces volume or renegotiates terms, the revenue impact would be immediate and significant.

**Recommended actions**
- Actively onboard more mid-sized agents
- Monitor agent performance monthly to flag early warning signs
- Strengthen direct booking incentives to dilute channel dependency

---

### 4. OTA Dependence

OTAs are the largest revenue source for the resort. While they deliver volume, they also bring **higher commissions and higher cancellation rates**.

**Recommended actions**
- Introduce loyalty perks for direct bookers (late checkout, breakfast, upgrades)
- Offer early-bird discounts exclusive to direct channels
- Retarget website visitors who abandon bookings

**Goal:** Shift **5–10%** of bookings from OTA to direct to deliver a measurable margin improvement.

---

## City Hotel

**Total Revenue:** €14.39M  
**Cancellation Rate:** 42%  
**Average Daily Rate:** €107.52  
**Average Stay:** 2.94 days  

---

### 1. Corporate Bookings — Year-Round Stability

Corporate bookings account for  **2.8% of revenue (€400k)** so there is room for growth . The corporate cancellation rate is **23%**, compared to **42% overall**, and demand is evenly distributed throughout the year.

This makes corporate bookings nearly **twice as reliable** as the average booking and particularly valuable in a high-cancellation environment.

**Recommended actions**
- Introduce fixed-rate corporate contracts for midweek stays
- Target nearby offices and conference venues
- Secure annual agreements with **local companies**

**Expected outcome:** More predictable occupancy and lower volatility.

---

### 2. Cancellation Is the Core Problem

A **42% cancellation rate** is the single biggest issue facing the City Hotel. The root cause is heavy OTA reliance.

**Recommended actions**
- Pilot stricter cancellation windows on peak dates
- Introduce a non-refundable rate tier (10–15% discount)
- Flag high-risk bookings and contact guests **48 hours pre-arrival**



---

### 3. Revenue Concentration Risk

One company contributes **€7M**, nearly half of total revenue. A small number of agents also account for a disproportionate share of bookings.

This creates exposure to budget cuts, renegotiation pressure, and sudden demand shocks.

**Recommended actions**
- Diversify the corporate client base aggressively
- Grow direct and small-agent channels
- Implement a monthly review of the top five accounts


---

## Cross-Hotel Themes

### 1. Corporate Is Underused
Both hotels generate 3% of revenue from corporate bookings, yet cancellation rates are just **20–23%**.

**Action:** Assign dedicated ownership to corporate sales.

### 2. OTA Dependence Drives Volatility
OTAs dominate both hotels, increasing cancellations and compressing margins.

**Action:** Invest in direct booking infrastructure, retargeting, and loyalty programs.

### 3. Seasonality Can Be Smoothed
The Resort peaks in Summer, while the City Hotel is more balanced. Both have clear shoulder-season opportunities.

**Action:** Coordinate Spring and Autumn campaigns targeting corporate, Ireland, and the UK.

## Resort vs City Hotel — Key Insights & Strategic Takeaways  
**Analysis period:** July 2015 – August 2017

This comparison highlights two fundamentally different hotel business models. While both properties generate similar total revenue, the drivers, risks, and growth opportunities are very different.

---

### Performance Snapshot

| Metric | City Hotel | Resort Hotel |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| Total Revenue | €14.39M | €11.45M |
| Avg Daily Rate (ADR) | €107.52 | €96.16 |
| Total Bookings | 45,434 | 26,854 |
| Avg Length of Stay | 2.94 days | 4.23 days |
| Cancellation Rate | 42% | 28% |

---

### 1. Revenue Model Differences

The City Hotel outperforms the Resort in total revenue primarily due to **volume**, not pricing. It handles far more bookings, but with shorter stays and significantly higher cancellations.

The Resort Hotel operates a **lower-volume, longer-stay model**, extracting most of its value during peak summer months.

**Insight:**  
The City Hotel is a high-throughput operation, while the Resort is a yield-driven, seasonally concentrated business.

---

### 2. Pricing Power & Seasonality

- The City Hotel maintains a **consistently higher ADR** throughout the year.
- The Resort Hotel experiences sharp ADR spikes in Summer but significant drops in Winter.

Seasonal ADR analysis shows:
- Resort Summer ADR peaks around €154
- Resort Winter ADR falls to €62
- City ADR remains comparatively stable across all seasons

**Insight:**  
The Resort relies heavily on peak-season pricing, while the City Hotel benefits from structurally strong, year-round demand.

---

### 3. Cancellation Risk — City Hotel Weakness

The City Hotel’s **42% cancellation rate** is its most critical issue, far exceeding the Resort’s 28%.


**Insight:**  
Demand is not the City Hotel’s problem — **booking quality is**. High cancellations significantly reduce realised revenue and forecasting accuracy.

---

### 4. Distribution Channel Dependence

Both hotels rely heavily on intermediaries:
- Online TA dominates revenue for both properties
- City Hotel fairs slightly better with direct bookings

**Insight:**  
OTA dependence increases commission costs and cancellation risk, particularly for the City Hotel.

---


### 6. Conversion & Stay Realisation

Reservation status data indicates:
- The Resort converts a higher proportion of bookings into completed stays
- The City Hotel loses more revenue post-booking due to cancellations

**Insight:**  
The City Hotel leaks value after the booking is made, while the Resort primarily struggles with demand outside peak periods.

---

### Strategic Summary

**City Hotel**
- Strong fundamentals: high ADR, strong year-round demand
- Core issue: cancellations and channel quality
- Largest upside comes from reducing cancellation leakage and improving booking commitment

**Resort Hotel**
- Highly seasonal, summer-driven revenue
- Strong peak pricing power
- Growth opportunity lies in filling Spring and Autumn with stable segments such as Corporate and high-value source markets

---

### Key Cross-Hotel Insight

> The City Hotel loses revenue *after* bookings are made.  
> The Resort Hotel loses revenue *before* bookings are made (outside Summer).
